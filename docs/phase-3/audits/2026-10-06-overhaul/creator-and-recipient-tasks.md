# Creator and recipient acceptance tasks

**Status: Prepared; participant sessions pending.** Use the practical acceptance template in this directory to record observations against the tested build. These scripts do not establish that the app is understandable to children or that a workplace procedure is safe.

The [print sample set](print-samples/README.md) supplies editable workplace/routine JSON and digitally inspected actual-size label, A6, A4, A3 and custom SVG/PDF files. Use them to prepare equipment; participants' familiar routines and required warnings supply the acceptance tasks.

## Before the session

Choose a real, familiar task with the creator and recipient. Record the intended actions, required warning, usual support, setting, and viewing distance before opening the app. The editable Prepare onion and Ready to draw examples can demonstrate controls; the participant's actual routine supplies the acceptance task. Use participant codes rather than identifying information. Arrange the session through the people who normally support the participant.

Ask the creator to make three short visual steps in their own words. Tell them they can ask for help. Do not explain where every control is before observing the first attempt; record help when supplied. Stop or adapt a task if the participant wants to stop. This is an observation of the product, not an assessment of the participant.

## Workplace or kitchen creator

1. Start in My guides and create a new sequence for the agreed repeated task. Give it a useful title.
2. Find and add pictures for three steps. Switch libraries when needed. Record search terms and any picture whose meaning is unclear.
3. Change a picture's label, add a required warning, and add a quantity or time only where the task needs one.
4. Move a picture using the named move controls. Remove one picture by mistake, then recover it with Undo.
5. Open Read. Explain what the recipient will see in Pictures + labels and Pictures only. Return to the same picture in editing.
6. Prepare a 50 × 30 mm label for a suitably small group and an A4 guide. If the label does not fit, observe whether the creator understands the recovery choices. Do not shrink a rejected print outside the app to mark the task passed.
7. Download, reopen, and compare the file with the preview. Return to My guides and reopen the saved guide. Make a JSON backup.

Record independent completion, assistance, unsuccessful attempts, and recovery for each action. Include the exact picture IDs for ambiguous artwork so a graphics revision can be traced.

## Teacher, parent, and supported child

Use a familiar routine such as preparing to draw; record which adult and child actions are separate. Keep the participant's ordinary communication and support available.

1. Create a sequence with three familiar steps, using pictures and short labels chosen by the child where possible.
2. Ask the child to find one picture, add it, and open its label controls. Record adult help and whether the target step is understood.
3. Move a picture earlier or later, then undo that change. Observe whether focus and selection make the result understandable.
4. Read the routine together and return to editing. Compare labelled and picture-only content.
5. Create a choice board for familiar activities with a Help or Stop choice when appropriate. Observe whether the recipient treats the entries as alternatives, without an implied “then” order.
6. Prepare an A6 card and A4 guide. The adult can handle downloads/printing; record who completes each part.

Do not convert a supported completion into an unsupported completion. Record what help made the flow usable, along with controls or pictures that need improvement.

## Recipient interpretation

Show the agreed printed or independently opened result at its intended size and viewing distance. Use customary support. Before explaining the guide, invite the recipient to show or communicate what each picture, step, and warning means. Record their interpretation separately from the intended meaning. Actual performance of a physical task should follow the setting's usual supervision and procedures.

Compare Pictures + labels and Pictures only when useful. Record misunderstood order, warning meaning, quantities/times, or activity choices, including whether labels or another picture resolve the confusion. A required warning that is missed or misunderstood is a finding to fix and retest.

## Retest and evidence

Link the build identity, editable JSON, exported files, actual print measurements, device details, and completed observation record. Keep software screenshots separate from actual prints. Retest the affected action after a correction and record both attempts. One successful task supports that task and participant context; broader claims require broader evidence.
